FRESH AND DRIED PASTA

TRADITIONAL VARIETIES SUCH AS TAGLIATELLE, PAPPARDELLE OR STUFFED RAVIOLI.

Pasta is one of the most authentic and beloved symbols of Italian cuisine. Rooted in the culture and history of our country, it represents not just a food but a true ritual that brings generations together. Behind its simplicity lies a world of traditions, production techniques and varieties that make every dish a unique experience.

The origins of pasta are ancient and much debated. Although a popular legend credits Marco Polo with bringing pasta to Italy on his return from China in the 13th century, the historical reality is very different. Pasta was already part of the diet of the ancient Romans, who ate a dough of flour and water called "lagana", the ancestor of modern lasagne. However, it was in the Middle Ages that dried pasta production spread thanks to Arab merchants, who brought to Sicily the art of "triyah", a type of dried pasta perfect for storage and transport.

From southern Italy, pasta quickly spread throughout the country and, over the centuries, became an essential part of the Italian diet, with hundreds of different shapes.

Unlike industrial pasta, artisanal pasta is made using traditional techniques, with slow drying times and high-quality raw materials. Durum wheat semolina, often sourced from local farms, is kneaded with pure water and extruded through bronze dies. This process gives the pasta a rough, porous surface, ideal for holding sauces and ensuring a perfect bite.

One of the most important aspects of artisanal pasta is precisely its drying: while industrial pasta dries quickly at high temperatures, artisanal pasta rests for a long time at low temperatures. This method preserves the wheat's natural qualities, making the pasta more digestible and flavourful.

Italy is a mosaic of flavours and traditions, and every region has its own pasta speciality. Here are some of the most famous:

  • Pasta di Gragnano IGP (Campania) — Considered one of the best pastas in the world, it is made exclusively in Gragnano using high-quality wheat and pure water from local springs. Its slow drying and bronze extrusion give it a unique texture, perfect for holding sauces and ensuring a flawless bite.
  • Orecchiette (Puglia) — Small and concave, perfect with turnip tops or rustic sauces.
  • Trofie (Liguria) — Typical of Ligurian cuisine, they pair wonderfully with pesto.
  • Pici (Tuscany) — Similar to spaghetti but thicker, ideal with hearty sauces such as aglione.
  • Busiate (Sicily) — Hand-rolled, perfect with Trapani-style pesto.
  • Strangozzi (Umbria) — A long, rustic pasta that pairs very well with truffle or sausage.

Every pasta shape was designed with a specific purpose, to best bring out the flavours of local ingredients.

Surface

Cooking

Ingredients

Pasta is incredibly versatile and lends itself to countless preparations. It can be dressed with simple sauces like garlic, oil and chilli, or become the star of elaborate recipes such as lasagne or timbales. Either way, the secret to a great pasta dish lies in choosing good ingredients and taking care in the preparation.

What's more, every shape has its ideal sauce: creamy sauces go best with long pasta such as fettuccine, while meat- or vegetable-based sauces pair perfectly with short, ridged pasta, which holds the sauce better.

ITALIAN CHEESES

PARMIGIANO REGGIANO, GORGONZOLA, BURRATA AND MANY OTHER DELIGHTS.

The tradition of Italian cheeses: a legacy of flavour and authenticity. In our shop we're proud to offer a carefully curated selection of Italian cheeses, each chosen for its authenticity, quality and unique flavour. Our goal is to bring you the best of Italian cheese-making tradition, making sure every cheese is authentic and of the highest possible quality. We're also here to help you choose the perfect cheese for your taste and needs. Whether you're after something soft and creamy or a more aged, sharper cheese, our experts are ready to advise and guide you every step of the way. Come and discover our range of Italian cheeses, ideal for every occasion, from a special dinner to a simple snack.

Italy is recognised worldwide for its rich and varied culinary tradition, and one of the true pillars of this gastronomy is undoubtedly its cheeses. From the creamiest to the most aged, Italian cheeses aren't just about the taste of tradition — they're also the result of centuries of history, culture and artisanal technique. Below we explore some of Italy's most iconic cheeses, whose character and flavour have won over palates the world over.

Let's start with one of the most famous cheeses:

  • Parmigiano Reggiano DOP. Known as the "King of Cheeses", Parmigiano Reggiano is produced in specific areas of northern Italy, such as Parma, Reggio Emilia, Modena and parts of Bologna and Mantua. Its intense flavour, both savoury and umami, is ideal grated over pasta, risottos and salads. Parmigiano Reggiano carries the Protected Designation of Origin (DOP), guaranteeing its authenticity and quality.
  • Pecorino Romano DOP. Another Italian DOP cheese is Pecorino Romano, originally from central Italy, particularly Sardinia, Lazio and the Grosseto area. Made from sheep's milk, this cheese is known for its strong, savoury flavour, which makes it a key ingredient in classic Italian dishes such as pasta alla carbonara and cacio e pepe. Its firm texture makes it ideal for grating.
  • Ricotta Fresca. Fresh Ricotta is a soft, creamy cheese made from whey. Unlike other cheeses, ricotta isn't pressed, giving it a spongy texture and delicate flavour. This cheese is a staple of Italian cooking, used in everything from traditional lasagne to desserts such as Sicilian cannoli.
  • Salva Cremasco. Salva Cremasco is a cow's-milk cheese originally from Lombardy. Its semi-firm texture and mild, milky flavour make it a great choice for those who prefer less aged cheeses. It's usually enjoyed with jam or fresh fruit.
  • Fontal. Fontal is another cow's-milk cheese from northern Italy. With a soft yet firm texture, its flavour is slightly sweet and buttery, making it perfect with sandwiches, salads or simply enjoyed on its own with a bit of bread.
  • Pecorino Fiore Sardo. Pecorino Fiore Sardo is a sheep's-milk cheese produced in Sardinia. Its flavour is deep and slightly spicy, with a firm, dense texture. This cheese is used in a wide range of dishes and is ideal for those who love bolder flavours.
  • Provolone. Provolone is a semi-aged cow's-milk cheese known for its strong, slightly spicy flavour. It's mainly produced in southern Italy, in regions such as Campania and Calabria. Different ageing levels exist, giving it great versatility in the kitchen.
  • Pecorino al Tartufo. Pecorino al Tartufo is a variety of Pecorino enriched with truffle, a highly prized ingredient in Italian gastronomy. Its flavour is more complex, combining the intensity of truffle with the strong taste of Pecorino, making it a luxury choice for gourmet cheese lovers.
  • Caciotta di Capra al Timo. Caciotta di Capra al Timo is a soft, creamy goat's-milk cheese. The addition of thyme gives it a herbal aroma and flavour, ideal with bread or on a cheese board. Perfect for those looking for a fresh, delicate taste.
  • Scamorza Affumicata. Scamorza Affumicata is a smoked Italian cow's-milk cheese. Its flavour is soft, with a smoky touch that makes it unmistakable. It's ideal melted into dishes such as pizzas or pasta, but can also be enjoyed sliced, alongside salads or starters.
  • Mozzarella di Bufala. Mozzarella di Bufala is perhaps the most recognised Italian cheese in the world. This fresh cheese, made from buffalo milk, is famous for its creamy texture and delicate flavour. Perfect with Caprese salad, pizza, or simply enjoyed with a drizzle of olive oil and basil.
  • Fiordilatte. Fiordilatte is a variety of mozzarella made with cow's milk instead of buffalo milk. Although its flavour is slightly milder, its texture is just as creamy and perfect for dishes such as pizzas and salads. It's a fresh, light cheese, very popular in central and southern Italy.
  • Caciocavallo. Caciocavallo is a semi-firm cow's-milk cheese, mainly produced in southern Italy, in regions such as Sicily and Calabria. Its flavour is intense and slightly spicy, and it's traditionally hung to mature naturally, which gives it its distinctive taste.
  • Provolone del Monaco. Provolone del Monaco comes from Campania, particularly the Amalfi Coast area. This cow's-milk cheese is known for its strong, spicy flavour, and its ageing can last several months, allowing it to develop a complex flavour profile. It's ideal with meats and vegetables, or enjoyed with a good wine.
  • Gorgonzola. Gorgonzola is one of the best-known blue cheeses in the world, originally from Lombardy. Its flavour varies depending on how long it's aged, ranging from soft and creamy to sharper and spicier. Gorgonzola is perfect with salads, pasta, pizza, or even as an appetiser with walnuts and honey. There are two versions of Gorgonzola: Dolce, milder, and Piccante, with a more intense, pungent flavour.
  • Taleggio. Taleggio is a soft-paste cheese with a reddish, wrinkled rind, mainly produced in Lombardy. It has a delicate, fruity flavour, although its aroma is quite intense. This cheese melts beautifully, making it ideal for sauces and fondue, as well as being perfect on a cheese board.
  • Asiago. Asiago is a semi-firm cheese originally from Veneto. Depending on how long it's aged, it can be fresh and mild or more mature and sharp. Its flavour is slightly sweet and nutty, making it an excellent accompaniment to soups, salads and pasta. Asiago also has a DOP version, which guarantees its quality and origin.
  • Burrata. Burrata is a very popular fresh cheese from Puglia. Made from cow's or buffalo milk, it has a creamy interior that makes it truly unique. Burrata is excellent in salads, pasta, or simply paired with tomato and basil in a Caprese-style salad.
